Greenwich Online

Rework of student portal for the university's new distance learning platform

Role
Main Designer
Team
Product owners, developer
Tools
Figma, Squiz CMS, HTML, CSS

Overview

To support the launch of Greenwich Online, a new distance learning initiative, presented an opportunity to refresh the student portal experience.

The problem

The current student portal pages were outdated, lacked information hierarchy, not optimised for accessibility, overwhelmed users with excessive content and multiple portlets.

Information, unless it is organized, processed and available to the right people in a format for decision making, is a burden, not a benefit - William Pollard

Research

I researched competitor student portals, built user profiles, and used Microsoft Clarity to analyse engagement with the current portal. The data showed many unused portlets, low scroll depth and content overload that made information hard to find. Without addressing these issues, the new design would carry problems.

Insight 1

Only half of users reach past mid-point of the page, with lower engagement further down.

Insight 2

Average fold shows most users only see the first row of the portlets without scrolling.

Insight 3

Users often go straight to the ‘Search the library’ section.

Define

Problem statement

The existing student portal was cluttered, inaccessible and poorly structured, so reusing its design for the new distance learning portal would have made it hard for students to find what they needed.

Goals

Simplify content management, improve information hierarchy and optimise portal for accessibility.

Success metrics

The design has not yet been tested with users. Planned measures of success include task completion rate, time to find key information, scroll depth and portlet engagement in Clarity, and accessibility audit results.
